# Zone alerts on MOT17: plain YOLOv8n vs YOLOv8n + S3x

Generated by `apps/zone_alert/compare.py` on 2026-10-31 17:02. Evaluation halves of MOT17-04, MOT17-09 (frames n//2+2..n; the detectors were trained on the first halves). ByteTrack, seed 0, cross-fitted threshold per model and sequence. Hysteresis K = 5 frames.

Definitions (details in the docstring of `compare.py`):

- **GT episode:** a GT pedestrian's foot point is inside the zone for K frames, using every annotated frame whatever the visibility. Its start is the first of those frames and its confirmation the K-th. The episode is **scored** when the person is inside and at least 0.5 visible on at least K of its frames; episodes of people hidden for the whole stay are not scored.
- **Hit:** an INTRUSION whose track is matched (IoU >= 0.5) to the episode's person on one of the K frames up to the alert. **Delay** = alert frame - GT confirmation frame (0 = as early as a perfect tracker; negative = the model's foot point confirmed the entry before the GT foot point did).
- **False alert:** the alerting track is matched, on those K frames, to a GT person outside the zone, to a MOT17 'distractor' or 'reflection' box, or to nothing. Alerts on a real person without a scored episode (including MOT17 'static person' and 'person on vehicle' boxes) are **unscored**, neither hit nor false.
- **Duplicate:** a further INTRUSION for an already-alerted episode (usually a new track id after an identity switch).

## What the numbers say

- Far zones, strict attribution: plain alerted 39 and S3x 40 of 48 GT intrusion episodes (per sequence, plain vs S3x: MOT17-04 28 vs 27 of 33; MOT17-09 11 vs 13 of 15). This is not a clear gain for S3x: the difference is a few episodes or none.
- Far zones, alert quality (plain vs S3x): false alerts 42 vs 28, duplicates 28 vs 30, false loitering 2 vs 2. With the post-hoc IoU 0.3 association: alerted 43 vs 44, false 19 vs 9.
- Delay in the far zones: median 0.00 s (plain) and 0.00 s (S3x), p90 1.93 s and 2.06 s.
- Near zones (control): plain 11 and S3x 12 of 13 episodes, false alerts 1 and 0.

## Notes (read before quoting the numbers)

- Two sequences only (MOT17-04 and MOT17-09 evaluation halves: 17.5 s and 8.7 s), one training seed (0), one threshold per model and sequence from leave-one-sequence-out cross-fitting (never tuned on the scored sequence). No confidence intervals: the episode counts are small, so differences of a few episodes are not evidence on their own.
- The far zones are where S3x (finer horizontal stride on P3 for small, narrow objects) is expected to matter; the near zones are the control. In MOT17-09 the far-zone people are still large (see the height column), so that zone is a weak test.
- The zones were drawn from scene geometry before any model output was seen and were not changed afterwards. One consequence: the near zone of MOT17-04 ends 5 px above the image edge, so people whose feet are cut off by the bottom edge are outside it (the same for GT and models).
- Delays include time a person spends hidden behind others after entering: the GT stay starts when the annotated foot point enters, whether or not the person can be seen.
- Rule history: the first version of this report credited a hit when the track had matched the person at any frame of the episode (even after the alert), counted alerts on 'static person' boxes as false, and built GT episodes only from frames with visibility >= 0.5. A review found that these rules credited some hits to the wrong person and produced negative delays from the visibility filter, so all three were changed (this version). The first version reported far-zone alerted 38 vs 39 of 47, false alerts 24 vs 21 and duplicates 47 vs 37 (plain vs S3x).
- Offline: frames are read from disk at full speed and timed on a desktop GPU, not the Jetson; the end-to-end edge pipeline (camera capture -> TensorRT -> tracker -> alert) has not been measured yet.
