## Notes (read before quoting the numbers)

- Two sequences (MOT17-04 and MOT17-09 evaluation halves, 17.5 s and 8.7 s), one seed (0), one threshold per model; no confidence intervals. The walk-in counts per zone are small, so single-digit differences are not evidence.
- APPROACH is limited by its speed threshold on clean paths and by the tracks on the models. With GT paths the rule warns 4 of 15 walk-ins in the MOT17-04 far zone (the small-person zone) and 7 of 9 in the MOT17-09 one; pooled 11 of 24, of which 7 at least 0.5 s ahead. The models warn 5 (plain) and 4 (S3x) far-zone walk-ins (4 and 3 at least 0.5 s ahead). Lowering min_speed to 0.10 BH/s (post hoc, on this data) lifts the oracle to 16 but the models only to 6 / 6, with more false warnings (oracle 6, models 12 / 11); any new threshold would have to be chosen on held-out data.
- Walking speed is scene-dependent. In the MOT17-04 far band (foot rows 70-330) GT movers measure 0.25 BH/s (IQR 0.14-0.44, n 53) when moving sideways, which foreshortening does not affect, and 0.17 BH/s (IQR 0.15-0.19, n 134) along the viewing direction. Box height changes little with depth (fit: 158 px at row 200, 221 px at row 700): the camera looks steeply down, so foreshortening is mild and these people simply stroll. The 0.25 threshold is about the median moving person here, not a standing threshold (MOT17-09: movers 0.44 BH/s (IQR 0.34-0.55, n 147); brisk walking across the image would be about 0.8 BH/s). The median GT entry speed in that zone is 0.18 BH/s, so 10 of 15 walk-ins have no direction at the entry and fall back to INTRUSION.
- Few true approaches in an alarm direction: 32 scored walk-ins in the 4 zones; the rule on the GT trajectories labels 15 of them INTRUSION, 15 of those only by the fail-safe (direction unknown): 0 walk-ins entered in a configured alarm direction at a measurable speed. In the MOT17-09 far zone every walk-in with a measured direction moves right, along the pavement (6 right, 3 unknown). So the direction-gated APPROACH table has very few targets; the ungated table is the test of the APPROACH rule.
- False warnings on no person: 18 of the models' warnings (ungated, both models, all zones) are on tracks matched to no GT pedestrian at IoU >= 0.5. 14 overlap a pedestrian loosely (IoU 0.2-0.5), 1 a pedestrian already matched to another track (IoU >= 0.5), and 2 a MOT17 'static person' box (IoU >= 0.3): S3x track 94 in MOT17-04 switches between a person sitting by a planter and the person standing behind, so its foot point jumps. The fit-residual check (cut 0.075 body heights, set on MOT17-02) removes 2 (plain) and 1 (S3x) far-zone false warnings and no hit; it does not stop track 94, whose switched box stays for most of the fit window.
- The direction filter never turned a GT alarm into an ENTRY ('GT alarm -> ENTRY' is 0 for both models). Its disagreements are model alarms where the GT trajectory gives ENTRY: 11 (both models), 8 of them fail-safe alarms of tracks too young or too slow for a direction at the entry.
- Speed and direction are measured in the image plane, normalised by the box height (body heights / s); there is no ground calibration. Perspective bends straight ground paths and compresses far motion, so the predicted entry time is approximate, more so in the far zones.
- The GT decisions use the same rule on annotated trajectories, which include interpolated foot points of hidden people; 'agreement' measures whether the model's tracks reproduce the rule's decision, not whether the rule matches human intent.
- Directions and tripwires were fixed from the first evaluation frame only, before any output of the new logic was seen, and were not changed afterwards. Rule changes after the first evaluation (from a review): fit-residual check (cut from held-out MOT17-02), re-arming only after moving away from the zone, fitted foot point must be outside the zone, tripwire starting side confirmed over K frames and K frames beyond a segment end forget the side. That end rule cost one true crossing per model on MOT17-09 (feet passing 3-8 px beyond the line's top end), so the ends were extended by 0.25 box heights; this margin was chosen after seeing that miss, and it also changes which GT crossings count (MOT17-09: 4 scored instead of 3).
